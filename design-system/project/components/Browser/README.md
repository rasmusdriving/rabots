The bots work in a browser on the Mac where Pobots runs, logged in as the person. Pobots shows that browser live beside the chat, in the same panel an artifact opens in, and lets the person step in. In the chat, where a bot worked in it, one row; in the roster, one quiet line.

The words are always "the browser" for the whole thing and "page" for one page in it. A workspace (a group of pages for one piece of work) is called by the name its maker gave it. Never "tab", "task space", "stream", "screencast", "session", "CDP" or the browser's product name in anything the person reads. The person's own workspace is "Your page".

## The row (`pb-browser`)

One button at its place in the bot's answer, in the family of the helper row: flat on `fill`, as wide as the answer, one fixed height (52px, the helper row's), from left to right:

- **The mark** (`pb-browser-mark`): a small drawn window with a line across its top, on a soft `fill` disc. Never a globe from an icon set, never a face. While the workspace is open beside the chat, the disc takes `accent-soft` and the mark `accent-text`, as an open artifact's row does.
- **The name** (`pb-browser-name`): the workspace's name, `ink`, 600, 13px, cut with an ellipsis.
- **One muted line** (`pb-browser-last`): the page in front, "Surveys · app.example.com". It changes in place, at most about once a second, and fades in anew.
- **At the right** (`pb-browser-state`): "Open" with the thinking mark beside it while a bot's command runs in the workspace; "Open" quietly otherwise; "Closed · 14:20" once the workspace is gone. Then the mark for where it opens (a window with its right part marked), not when closed.

Rules:

- **It is work.** It folds away with the turn's work into "Worked for". But while its workspace is still open after the turn (the bot left it open because it needs the person, for a sign-in or a choice), it stays in sight with the answer, in its order, like an artifact's row. Once in sight it stays there until the chat is drawn again, also after the workspace closes: it turns "Closed" in its box and nothing moves under the person's eyes. Drawn again later (another bot and back, a reload), a closed row is in the fold. Chosen over moving it into the fold at the moment it closes, because that moment is not the person's: a row that leaves the answer while they read it is the jump they asked never to see.
- **One fixed height in every state.** Open, working, a new page, closed: the words change in place.
- One row per turn and workspace, at the place where the bot first worked there in that turn; the engine's lines of work for the single commands ("Worked in the browser") stay after it.
- Click or Enter opens the browser beside the chat on that workspace. A closed row is quiet (its name in `ink-muted`) and does nothing.
- It is one of the chat's line buttons: one Tab stop for all of them, Up and Down between them, the 2px focus ring. Escape on the row of the open workspace closes the panel.

## The panel (`pb-browser-panel`)

The artifact panel itself (see Artifact): the same place in the rail's column, the same width and divider, "Fill the main area", the way back at 800 px, "Needs you 2" in the top line, Escape, the focus back to the row, and the chat keeping its place. Opening the browser while an artifact is open swaps the content of the one panel, and the other way round; the panel and its divider stay. The browser is not one bot's: the panel stays open across bots, tabs and channels, and across a reload, and closes with "Close" or Escape.

- **The top line** (`pb-artifact-bar`, 56px): the workspace's name over one muted line with the page's title and host, changing in place. In the person's own page the muted line is an address line (`pb-browser-address`, a small well in `font-mono`): Enter goes there and takes control by itself, since the page is theirs; Escape puts the page's address back. Only http and https addresses: anything else is refused in one plain sentence in the state line ("Only addresses that start with http or https can be opened here."). In a bot's workspace the address is only shown.
- **"Take control"** (`pb-browser-take`), a word: control is a state the person must see. In control it reads "Hand back" on `accent-soft` with `accent-text`.
- **The small controls**, icons with their words on hover and for screen readers, as in the artifact panel: "Back", "Forward", "Load again" (only in control), "Other work in the browser" (a small menu: the bots' workspaces with the bot's small face, "Your page", and under a quiet heading "Other work in the browser" the work of other programs; in your own page also "Close your page"), "Open in a new tab" (your own page only), "Narrower", "Wider" (hidden when the panel is too narrow for them; the divider does the same), "Fill the main area" or "Show the chat beside it", "Close". Menus float on `surface-overlay` with `shadow-float`; Arrow keys move, Enter chooses, Escape closes the menu and gives the focus back to its control.
- **The state line** (`pb-browser-stateline`), one quiet line under the top line that keeps its height with or without words: "Po is working here" while a bot's command runs there; "You are in control." and, with a bot working there, "You are in control. Po is not stopped." (in `accent-text`); "Take control to click and type" after the first click without control; "Po closed this" when the workspace closed while open ("Your page was closed" for your own). Said once, here.
- **The picture** (`pb-browser-picture`) fills the rest, on `frame-ground`, as large as the panel allows with its proportions kept, never larger than the page's own size, centred at the top. Each picture is drawn once it is decoded, over the one before: the panel never shows an empty moment.
- **As sharp as the real page.** The window tells the engine how many screen pixels one page pixel covers where the picture is drawn (the screen's own pixel ratio times how much the panel scales the page), when watching starts and again when the panel's size or the screen's pixel ratio changes, at most a few times a second while the divider is dragged. While the page changes, the pictures stay light and quick (a JPEG, never above the page's own size). Once the page has stood still for about half a second, one more picture comes at exactly the size it is drawn, up to twice the page's own size: a PNG, or a WebP when a PNG would be large (a page full of photos). The canvas is that picture's own size and is drawn pixel for pixel; its drawn size does not change, so the swap from the light picture to the sharp one moves nothing. Positions for input stay in the page's own pixels. Until the first picture: "Opening the browser" in `ink-muted`, only after a moment (`dur-slow`). When the browser cannot be reached: the engine's sentence with "Try again". When the connection goes, the last picture stays and the panel asks to watch again by itself when it comes back.
- **Closed while open**: the last picture stays, dimmed; "Take control" is off; the pickers and "Close" stay, so the person can go elsewhere.
- **Its own width.** The browser's width is remembered on its own, apart from an artifact's, and starts as wide as the divider allows (the chat at its readable minimum): a page is hard to read at half size. Swapping between an artifact and the browser changes the width in one step, with the chat's place kept as for every change of width. Narrower, Wider and the divider step from the width shown.
- **The address follows the page.** The muted line, and the address line in your own page, show the page the picture shows, within a moment of every move (a typed address, a link, back, forward). A new page with no address yet shows nothing there (the address line its placeholder, "Type an address"); never the browser's own address for a new page, and never its words for one.

## The row of pages (`pb-browser-tabs`)

Between the top line and the state line, one place per page of the workspace, in the browser's order. The person sees every page and picks one; there is no page menu.

- **Always there, one height.** The row is 36px high with one page too, so a page that opens or closes never moves the picture: it changes the row in place, a new page fading in where it belongs.
- **Each page** (`pb-browser-tab`, 28px, `radius-sm`) shows its title, cut with an ellipsis; the full title and the host on hover. A new page with no address yet is "A new page". The page shown is marked as a chosen segment is: `fill-strong` and weight 600. No border, no coloured stripe.
- **Many pages** shrink to 96px each, then the row scrolls sideways inside itself (a turning wheel moves it too, the scrollbar is hidden); nothing wraps, the window never scrolls sideways, and the page shown is kept in view.
- **Choosing a page** shows it. Watching goes on and control is kept. The page comes to the front in the browser, as a click on its tab would, also without control: in the bots' browser any look at a page (even a picture of it) makes it the page in front, so a page cannot be shown and left behind. The picture then follows the page in front, so a bot that goes back to its own page, or opens a new one, is followed there; nothing pulls a page back to the front behind the bot. So the page shown is always the page in front in the browser, and the row needs no mark for "in front elsewhere": it would only flash between two pictures.
- **A page's question belongs to its page.** A drop-down's choices and a page's question (see below) show only while their page is shown. A page whose question waits while another is shown carries the waiting mark (`pb-mark-waiting`) beside its title, and its name says "the page asks something" to a screen reader. The window learns of a question when its page is shown; a page that starts asking while another is shown is not marked (the protocol says nothing about other pages).
- **Your page** has a quiet add control at the end of the row, "Open a new page", which opens a new page in front and puts the focus in the address line; each page has a close control ("Close this page", a small cross) that shows on hover and on focus, never on the last page. Both take control by themselves, as the address line does. In a bot's workspace, and in other work in the browser, neither is there.
- **Keyboard.** The row is one stop: the page shown. Left and Right move between pages, Home and End go to the first and the last, Enter or Space shows the focused page; in your page Delete (or Backspace) closes the focused page and the focus goes to the page beside it. A screen reader hears a tab list named "Pages", each page a tab; the picture is the panel it controls.
- **Closed while open**: the row stays as it was, quiet, and choosing does nothing.

## What the page shows outside its picture (`pb-browser-over`)

The browser on the Mac draws two things outside the page, where the picture cannot show them: a page's own question (alert, confirm, prompt), and the open list of a drop-down list. The engine says so (`browser.prompt`), and the app draws them on a layer exactly over the picture, clipped by the panel. Everyone who watches sees them; only the person in control can answer.

- **The page asks** (`pb-browser-ask`): a small raised card at the top in the middle of the picture, arriving with the usual calm fade (`pb-arrive`). "The page asks" in `ink-faint`, the page's message (a few lines, the rest scrolling), a text field for a prompt with the page's own default text, and "OK" and "Cancel" (an alert has only "OK"). These two words are the page's, so they are kept. The picture under it is dimmed a little (`data-asked`), so it reads as waiting. In control it takes the focus (the field, else "OK"); Enter in the field is "OK", Escape is "Cancel". Without control the buttons are off and one quiet line says "Take control to answer"; it does not take the focus. A screen reader hears it as an alert dialog named "The page asks".
- **The choices of a drop-down list** (`pb-browser-choices`): a press on a list in the page (or Space, Enter, Up, Down or Alt+Down while one has the focus) shows its choices as one of the app's own menus, never the page's: under the list's box, placed and sized with the picture's scale (never narrower than 200px), above it when there is more room there, never outside the panel, scrolling inside when long. The chosen option is checked, a disabled option is quiet and skipped, a group heading stands over its options. Up, Down, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down move; typing the first letters goes to an option; Enter, Space or a click chooses; Escape or Tab closes without choosing, and that Escape is not passed to the page. While the choices are open nothing else goes to the page; a press elsewhere closes them. They close too when control is handed back, when the watch ends, and when the engine says they are gone (the page moved on). After a choice the focus goes back to the picture.
- A key's release only follows its press: a key whose press the menu or the card took never reaches the page as a lone release.

## Stepping in

- Not in control, a click on the picture only takes the focus. In control, the pointer and the keys go to the page while the pointer is over the picture or the picture has the focus (the picture takes the focus when the pointer comes over it, unless the person is typing somewhere else). Positions go in the page's own pixels; moves at most about 30 times a second, what happens in between in one batch, in order.
- Keys: everything the page needs goes to it, Tab and Shift+Tab, Enter, Backspace, Delete, the arrows, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down included, and the editing shortcuts with Ctrl as well as the command key. The laptop's own shortcuts that would leave the page (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+T, Ctrl+N, Ctrl+L, Ctrl+R, Ctrl+Tab, F5, F11, F12, Alt with the arrows) are left to the laptop's browser. A paste sends the laptop's clipboard text at once; Ctrl+C and Ctrl+X pass the keys on and then put what the page selected on the laptop's clipboard.
- **When the clipboard is refused.** The app keeps the last text it copied or cut from the page. When the laptop's browser refuses to write it to the clipboard, the state line says once "Copied inside this page only. Your browser did not allow the clipboard.", and the next paste in the panel uses the kept text: the clipboard holds something older then, or nothing. A copy elsewhere in Pobots, or the window losing the focus (the person may copy in another program), makes the laptop's clipboard count again.
- **Escape, without a trap.** The page needs Escape too, so one Escape over the picture goes to the page. Escape twice quickly, or once with the pointer away from the picture, leaves the picture for "Hand back"; from there Escape closes the panel as usual. Without control the picture keeps no keys at all.
- The picture is a focus stop with the 2px ring; it is read out as "The page" with what control means.

## The roster line (`pb-browser-line`)

At the bottom of the roster, with the machines, only while the browser can be reached: "Browser" and how much is open in `font-mono` ("2 open", nothing when none). No badge, no colour. It opens the browser beside the chat on the open bot's workspace when it has one, else on the newest bot workspace, else on your own page (made on demand). While the browser is open it is marked like an open channel row. In a roster that shows faces only it is the small drawn window on a disc, its words read out.

## Pages on Claude

A page on Claude (an artifact's link, "Open on Claude") opens in its own window beside Pobots, also when the browser can be reached (since step 2g's third round: the live picture was not sharp enough for reading a document). Nothing in the app opens a page on Claude in the browser panel by itself; the person can type or paste any address in "Your page".
